Who I Am

I work as an AI training contractor on Micro1 and Mercor, which both do AI training work for the big AI companies. Most of my work is writing: building "golden" rubrics, prompts and model responses in generalist and humanities topics (literature, history, psychology), and checking other contributors' rubrics for quality. I've completed Prompt Academy and Rubric Academy training and a red-teaming workshop.

I've also done:

  • Evaluation: rating AI emotional-support responses for safety, empathy, avoiding diagnosis and knowing when to escalate; comparing personalized vs. standard AI answers.
  • Annotation: audio transcription and speaker labelling, OCR (drawing boxes and transcribing text), checking how faithfully AI reproduces Office documents, and tagging/counting things in video, images and audio.

I'm not an attorney, physician or engineer, so for those fields I can only describe the process, not the specialist work itself.

How the AI Interview Works, and How to Prepare

Both platforms interviewed me with an AI interviewer, not a person. On Micro1 it was thorough, and the questions were clearly tied to the role I applied for. It wasn't hard, but it rewarded preparation. Mercor's felt slightly easier. The same preparation works for both.

This is the advice I'd give a friend. It's general preparation advice, not inside information about what will be asked:

  1. Read the role posting carefully. Note the field, the skills listed and the kind of tasks described. That's your study guide.
  2. Review your fundamentals. Brush up on the core concepts of your field that the role mentions, as if you had to explain them to a smart beginner.
  3. Practice explaining your reasoning out loud. AI training work is about judgement: why one answer is better than another. Get comfortable talking through your thinking step by step.
  4. Set up a quiet space. Good internet and no interruptions. If your interview uses your mic or camera, test them beforehand.
  5. Be specific and honest. Use real examples from your own work. Don't overstate experience. The role match depends on it.

Getting Offers on Mercor

  • Take as many assessments as you can. Mercor's assessments qualify you for roles automatically, and many offers come this way without an interview. The more you do, the better your chances. These qualifying assessments aren't paid, but they're worth it.
  • Do the mini certifications. Mercor offers short courses (for example prompt engineering and agentic training) that qualify you for work.

How to Keep the Work Coming

Do good, thorough work. These projects exist to produce good training data for the AI companies. Good data keeps the client happy, and a happy client keeps the project running, which means more work for you.

  • Don't stretch tasks to bill more time.
  • Don't rush through tasks without being thorough.

Both shortcuts hurt the data, and they're usually how projects end early.

Timeline

From applying to first paid work, for me: Micro1 usually took one week to one month; Mercor took under a week. It depends on the role and how quickly projects start. Your timeline may differ.

Pay

  • Pay is set per role and shown on each job posting. Rates vary a lot by field, so check the posting rather than trusting any single number you see online.
  • Payments on both have been very reliable. I've never had an issue on either.
  • On both, training, assessments and quizzes are paid only inside a project, once you've onboarded. The qualifying assessments you take before that aren't paid.
  • Mercor: pay has been about the same as Micro1 for me.

FAQ

Are Micro1 and Mercor legit?

I can only speak for myself: I've done paid work on both, and payments have been very reliable on both, never an issue. That's my experience, not a guarantee for every role or country.

One caution: apply only through the platforms' own sites. Never pay anyone for a "guaranteed" role, and be wary of people messaging you claiming to be their recruiters.

Micro1 or Mercor: which is better?

In my experience, Micro1 has more roles, with new ones added daily, while Mercor gets you into a role faster. Pay has been about the same for me, and payments on both have been very reliable. Applying to both gives you the best of each.

How do I get offers on Mercor without an interview?

Take as many of Mercor's assessments as you can. They qualify you for roles automatically, and many offers come that way without an interview. Mercor's mini certifications (for example prompt engineering and agentic training) also qualify you for work.

Are assessments and training paid?

On both platforms, training, assessments and quizzes are paid only inside a project, once you've onboarded. The qualifying assessments you take before that aren't paid.

Is the AI interview hard?

For me it wasn't hard, but it was thorough and specific to the role. Mercor's felt slightly easier than Micro1's. Preparation makes the difference. See the prep tips above.

How long until I get paid work?

In my case, one week to one month on Micro1 and under a week on Mercor. It varies by role.

How much does it pay?

It depends on the role. The rate is listed on each posting. I won't quote a number here because rates change and differ by field.
